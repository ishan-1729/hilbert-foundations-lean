# Faithfulness: how *The Foundations of Geometry* is read

A proof checked by Lean establishes a Lean statement. Whether that statement is Hilbert's is a separate question, and this file records how it was answered. Every axiom, definition, theorem and claim of the book is a unit block `## [id] Title` in `HilbertFoundations/*.lean`, which quotes Townsend's translation, says in a **Lean reading** paragraph how the words are formalized, and ends with a **Status** line. This file collects what applies throughout: the unit discipline, the meaning of the statuses, the encoding of the primitive notions and of the axioms in [`HilbertFoundations/Basic.lean`](../HilbertFoundations/Basic.lean), every correction, every reading, every open statement, the statements proved in another form than Hilbert's, and what is not formalized.

## Units

A unit is a comment block followed by its Lean declarations:

```
## [id] Title
*(§ N)*
> Hilbert's words
**Lean reading.** how they are formalized
**Status.** one of the six statuses
```

- **Quotation.** The words are those of `data/foundations.json`, which `scripts/extract_source.py` extracts from the Project Gutenberg LaTeX source (eBook #17384). A long passage is cut with `…`. The 430 quotations consist of 518 fragments between ellipses, and each fragment occurs literally, up to white space, in the text of `data/foundations.json`. Mathematics stays in TeX, a figure is `[Fig. N]`, and a footnote is inlined as `[Note: …]`, as in the extracted text.
- **Lean reading.** It names the declarations, says which axiom classes are assumed, writes out the hypotheses that Hilbert leaves tacit, and states every decision between readings. Where a proof is not Hilbert's, or a step of his argument is not formalized, it says so.
- **Status.** Exactly one of the six strings of the table above, with nothing after it.
- **Identifiers.** `ax.I1`, …, `ax.I7`, `ax.II1`, …, `ax.II5`, `ax.III`, `ax.IV1`, …, `ax.IV6`, `ax.V` and `ax.completeness` are the 21 axioms of Chapter I. `th.1`, …, `th.44` are the 44 numbered theorems. `sN.<slug>` is a statement of § N that the book does not label (324 units). `intro.<slug>` (5), `concl.<slug>` (15) and `app.<slug>` (21, among them the axioms `app.axiom1`, `app.axiom2`, `app.axiom3` of the appendix) belong to the introduction, the conclusion and the appendix.
- **Where a unit lives.** Unit blocks are in 14 files: `Basic.lean` and the 13 chapter files. A unit is filed under the section in which its statement occurs, not under the file name: the notions of § 4 that the axioms of congruence need (`s4.side_of_point`, `s4.half_ray`, `s4.side_of_line`) are in `Basic.lean`, and the laws of § 13 are in `Analytic.lean`. The other 114 files of `HilbertFoundations/` hold no unit blocks: 105 helper modules `*Aux*.lean` and the nine files `OrderPolygon.lean`, `CongruenceClose.lean`, `CompletenessClose.lean`, `CoordinatesClose.lean`, `ConstructionsClose.lean`, `CommentaryClose.lean`, `JordanCurveClose.lean`, `Theorem42Close.lean`, `TwoVariablesClose.lean`. The four Lean files of the directory `JordanPick/` are not files of the formalization, and neither are the modules of the dependencies Tau Ceti and `hilbert17`, which are not in the repository (see Third-party code).
- **Units proved in another file.** For 17 units the theorem that proves the statement is in one of those nine files. For 14 of them the reason is that the proof needs a later chapter: `th.6` (`polygonTheorem`), `th.18` (`theorem18`, `theorem18_distinct`), `s7.circle_properties` (`circlePropertiesClaim`), `s8.bolzano` (`completenessGivesBolzano`), `s8.real_numbers` (`completenessGivesReals`), `s17.space` (`spaceCoordinatesClaim`), `s17.ideal` (`idealElementsClaim`), `s30.any_dimension` (`anyDimensionClaim`), `th.43` (`theorem43`), `s39.regular_polygons` (`regularPolygonsClaim`), `s39.malfatti` (`malfattiClaim`), `concl.non_legendrian` (`nonLegendrianGeometryExists`), `concl.legendre_archimedes` (`legendreNeedsArchimedes`), `concl.semi_euclidean` (`semiEuclideanGeometryExists`). The fifteenth is `app.jordan_theorem` (`jordanCurveTheorem`, in `JordanCurveClose.lean`), whose proof uses the copied library `JordanPick` (see Third-party code). The sixteenth is `th.42` (`theorem42`, in `Theorem42Close.lean`), whose proof uses the Hasse–Minkowski theorem of the library Tau Ceti, a dependency of the package (see Third-party code); the proof is in a file of its own, so that this file is the only one that imports the dependency. The seventeenth is `s38.two_variables` (`twoVariableClaim`, in `TwoVariablesClose.lean`), whose proof uses Artin's theorem as proved in the package `hilbert17`, a second dependency (see Third-party code); it is in a file of its own for the same reason. The unit block states the claim as a `Prop` and names the theorem. The last column of `docs/UNITS.md` lists only the declarations that follow the block in its own file, so for these units the proving theorem is found through the Lean reading.

**The unit map and its check.** `scripts/unit_table.py` reads every `## [id]` block and writes `docs/UNITS.md`: the counts by status, and for each unit its section, the opening words of the quotation, the status and the declarations that follow the block. With `--check` it exits with an error if a unit has no status or a placeholder for one, if one of the 68 labelled statements of `data/foundations.json` (24 axioms, 44 theorems) has no unit, if an identifier occurs twice, or if a unit whose status begins with `proved` is followed by a declaration that uses `sorry`. The CI workflow runs the build, the axiom audit and this check, and fails if the regenerated `docs/UNITS.md` differs from the committed one. The check does not test whether a Lean statement says what Hilbert says. That is the subject of the rest of this file.

## Statuses

- **`proved`.** The Lean reading is the evident one, and the statement is proved with the axiom classes Hilbert names, or fewer. Hypotheses that every reader supplies are written out: "two points" are distinct, a triangle has non-collinear vertices, "two lines" are different lines. A misprint that does not affect the mathematics is noted and the status stays `proved`.
- **`proved (reading)`.** The words admit more than one precise statement, or use a notion the book does not define, or describe an object for which the proof is carried out in a different but equivalent or analogous form (a model, a configuration, a co-ordinate plane). The Lean reading says which statement is proved, why, and what is left out.
- **`proved (corrected)`.** As printed, the claim is false, or too imprecise to be true or false. The Lean reading says exactly what is wrong, the corrected statement is proved, and in most cases a declaration refutes the printed form (see Corrections).
- **`definition`.** A definition or an axiom. The 24 labelled axioms and the 17 laws `s13.law1`, …, `s13.law17` of § 13 have this status: an axiom is a `Prop`-valued definition and a field of a class. Many definition units come with lemmas that justify the encoding.
- **`remark`.** Prose without a mathematical statement to prove: a classification of the axioms, an announcement, a historical note, a programme.
- **`stated (open here)`.** The claim is a `Prop`-valued definition. It is not proved, it is never assumed through `sorry` or `axiom`, and the unit says what is proved towards it and what is missing.

The line between a reading and a correction is a judgement, made unit by unit. A correction changes the mathematics of the printed sentence: Theorem 21 needs `A ≠ B`. A reading repairs the wording or the encoding: Theorem 35 carries `[Nonempty L]` because the Lean class of plane geometry admits a structure that Hilbert's words exclude, and Theorem 38 is read with the asymmetry of law 13 that "the other number is said to be the smaller" presupposes.

## Conventions

The conventions are fixed in the module docstring and the 47 units of [`HilbertFoundations/Basic.lean`](../HilbertFoundations/Basic.lean) (36 definitions, 8 remarks, 3 proved). The axioms are those of this edition, which differ from the later ones: II 1 has no distinctness clause, II 4 is the four-point axiom, IV 1 and IV 4 contain "every segment (angle) is congruent to itself", IV 6 concludes both remaining angles, V is Archimedes's axiom, and the Axiom of Completeness is introduced in a remark that Hilbert added for the French translation (unit `ax.completeness`, status `definition`).

### Primitive notions

- **Points, lines, planes** are three types `Point`, `Line`, `Plane`. Lines and planes are primitive objects, not sets of points (`s1.elements`).
- **Incidence.** `HasLines Point Line` gives `LiesOn : Point → Line → Prop` and `HasPlanes Point Plane` gives `LiesIn : Point → Plane → Prop`, both written `A ∈ a`. The line and plane types are `outParam`s: a point type has one system of lines and planes.
- **Between.** `HasBetween Point` gives `Between A B C`, "`B` lies between `A` and `C`".
- **Congruent.** `HasSegCong Point` gives `SegCong A B A' B'` (`AB ≡ A'B'`) and `HasAngCong Point` gives `AngCong A O B A' O' B'` (`∠AOB ≡ ∠A'O'B'`). These are relations on tuples of points.
- **Derived notions.** `Collinear A B C` and `Coplanar A B C D` say that some line, or some plane, contains the points. `Meets a b` says that two lines have a common point. `Parallel a b` is `¬Meets a b`, so a line is not parallel to itself; `ParallelIn α a b` is the same for lines of a plane `α`. "Continuous" has no relation of its own: it is axiom V and the Axiom of Completeness (`s1.relations`).
- **Degenerate tuples are junk.** Every axiom is quantified over genuine objects only: a segment has two distinct extremities, an angle has non-collinear `A`, `O`, `B`. The values of `SegCong` and `AngCong` on other tuples are unconstrained, except by `segCong_swap`. Betweenness is not junk-tolerant: `Between A B C` holds only for three distinct points of a line.

### How the defined notions are encoded

| Hilbert | Lean | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| segment `AB` or `BA` (`s3.segment`) | a pair of distinct points; `Seg A B` is the set of points between them, `SegWithEnds A B` adds the extremities | Symmetry in the extremities is `seg_comm` for the points and the field `segCong_swap` for congruence (`s6.segment_congruence`). |
| same side of a point `O` (`s4.side_of_point`) | `SameSidePt O A A'`: `A ≠ O`, `A' ≠ O`, `Collinear A O A'`, `¬Between A O A'` | Hilbert's auxiliary point `B` is dropped (it exists by II 2), and `A' = A` is allowed. |
| half-ray (`s4.half_ray`) | `ray O A`, the set of points on the same side of `O` as `A`; `IsHalfRay O h` | A half-ray is a set of points. |
| sides of a line (`s4.side_of_line`) | `SameSide a A B`: `A, B ∉ a` and no point of `a` lies between them; `OppositeSides a A B`: some point of `a` does | Hilbert defines the sides by the regions of Theorem 5, which is proved later; they are defined directly, and `theorem5_sides`, `sides_of_line_iff` (Order.lean) prove that they are the regions of Theorem 5. |
| angle `∠(h, k)` (`s6.angle`) | a non-collinear triple `A, O, B`, with `h = ray O A`, `k = ray O B` | `SameAngle A O B C D` says that two triples give the same unordered pair of half-rays. That congruence is a relation between angles is the field `angCong_wd`. |
| interior of an angle (`s6.interior`) | `Interior A O B`: the points on the side of `OA` where `B` lies and on the side of `OB` where `A` lies | Hilbert defines it as one of two regions whose existence he asserts; it is defined directly, as in later editions. The two regions and the convexity of the interior are `s6.angle_regions`; that the interior depends only on the angle, not on the points chosen on its sides, is `s6.interior_well_defined` (both in Congruence.lean). |
| triangle (`s6.angle_of_triangle`) | `IsTriangle A B C := ¬Collinear A B C` | The angle `∠BAC` is the triple `B, A, C`. |
| "equal" segments in axiom V (`s8.equality`) | `ArchimedesWith Point Equal` for an arbitrary relation `Equal` | `AxiomV` takes `Equal = SegCong`. Chapter VI uses the equality of § 24, `TrEqual` (equal segments are translates of each other). |

### The axioms as classes

Every axiom is a standalone `Prop` (`AxiomI1`, …, `AxiomV`, `PlaneComplete`, `SpaceComplete`), so that a model can be checked against a single axiom and "IV 6 fails" or "II 4 follows from the others" can be stated. The classes have one field per axiom.

| Class | Extends | Own fields | Hilbert |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Connection` | – | `ax_I1`, `ax_I2`, `ax_I7_line` | I 1–2 and the line clause of I 7 |
| `PlaneConnection` | `Connection` | `ax_I7_noncollinear` | group I of plane geometry |
| `SpaceConnection` | `Connection` | `ax_I3`, `ax_I4`, `ax_I5`, `ax_I6`, `ax_I7_plane`, `ax_I7_space` | I 1–7 |
| `LineOrder` | `Connection` | `between_onLine`, `ax_II1`, `ax_II2`, `ax_II3`, `ax_II4` | II 1–4 |
| `PlaneOrder` | `PlaneConnection`, `LineOrder` | `ax_II5` | I, II in the plane |
| `SpaceOrder` | `SpaceConnection`, `LineOrder` | `ax_II5` | I, II in space |
| `PlaneParallels` | `PlaneConnection` | `ax_III` | III in the plane |
| `SpaceParallels` | `SpaceConnection` | `ax_III` | III in each plane |
| `SegmentCongruence` | `LineOrder` | `segCong_swap`, `ax_IV1`, `ax_IV1_refl`, `ax_IV2`, `ax_IV3` | IV 1–3 |
| `AngleCongruence` | `LineOrder` | `angCong_wd`, `ax_IV4_refl`, `ax_IV5` | the parts of IV 4–5 that mention no plane |
| `PlaneAngleCongruence` | `PlaneOrder`, `AngleCongruence` | `ax_IV4` | IV 4–5 in the plane |
| `SpaceAngleCongruence` | `SpaceOrder`, `AngleCongruence` | `ax_IV4` | IV 4–5 in space |
| `TriangleCongruence` | `SegmentCongruence`, `AngleCongruence` | `ax_IV6` | IV 6 |
| `PlaneCongruence` | `PlaneAngleCongruence`, `TriangleCongruence` | – | I, II, IV in the plane |
| `SpaceCongruence` | `SpaceAngleCongruence`, `TriangleCongruence` | – | I, II, IV in space |
| `Archimedes` | `LineOrder` | `ax_V` | V, with congruent segments as equal segments |
| `PlaneGeometry` | `PlaneParallels`, `PlaneCongruence`, `Archimedes` | – | groups I–V, plane |
| `SpaceGeometry` | `SpaceParallels`, `SpaceCongruence`, `Archimedes` | – | groups I–V, space |

Three fields are not numbered axioms of Hilbert's. They express what his words presuppose:
- `between_onLine` (`BetweenOnLine`, unit `s3.between`): if `B` lies between `A` and `C`, the three are distinct points of one line. The axioms of this edition speak only of such points, and without it not even `Between A B C → A ≠ C` follows from II 1–5. Later editions put it into II 1; here II 1 keeps its form.
- `segCong_swap` (`SegCongSwap`): `AB ≡ A'B'` gives `BA ≡ A'B'` and `AB ≡ B'A'`, because a segment is "`AB` or `BA`".
- `angCong_wd` (`AngCongWellDefined`): for genuine angles, `AngCong` depends only on the two angles, not on the points chosen on their sides or on the order of the sides.

A subset of the axioms is a combination of classes. "I 1–2, II–III, IV 1–5, V" of Theorem 33 is `PlaneOrder`, `PlaneParallels`, `SegmentCongruence`, `PlaneAngleCongruence`, `Archimedes`, without `TriangleCongruence`.

### Plane geometry and space geometry

- The linear axioms (I 1–2, the line clause of I 7, II 1–4, IV 1–3, V) and the plane-free parts of IV 4–6 mention no plane. Their classes are shared by plane and space geometry.
- The plane axioms II 5, III and the laying-off clause of IV 4 have two versions: over `(Point, Line)`, where the plane is the set of all points (`AxiomII5`, `AxiomIII`, `AxiomIV4LayOff`), and over `(Point, Line, Plane)`, where the axiom is stated inside an arbitrary plane `α` (`AxiomII5Space`, `AxiomIIISpace`, `AxiomIV4LayOffSpace`).
- Group I of plane geometry is I 1–2 with the two clauses of I 7 that make sense in a plane: every line has two points, and there are three non-collinear points (`ExistsNoncollinear`). Hilbert's division into "plane axioms I 1–2" and "space axioms I 3–7" is not quite exact for this reason (`s2.plane_space_axioms`).
- A space geometry is not a plane geometry, but each of its planes is one. The points of a plane `α` and the lines lying in it, the subtypes `{A // A ∈ α}` and `{a // LiesInPlane a α}`, satisfy the plane classes: `planeConnection_of_space`, `planeOrder_of_space`, `planeParallels_of_space` (Order.lean, units `s2.plane_restriction`, `s3.plane_restriction`). Plane theorems are transferred to the planes of space through this restriction. Plane theorems of §§ 4–7 are stated in both forms, for example `theorem5` and `theorem5_space`, `theorem10` and `theorem10_space`.

### Reading decisions in the axioms

| Axiom | Decision |
|---|---|
| I 1, I 2 | I 1 is the existence and I 2 the uniqueness of the line through two distinct points, as in all later editions. Read literally, the "that is" clause of I 2 is a tautology. The line `AB` is `line A B h`, chosen by I 1. |
| I 3, I 4 | Existence and uniqueness of the plane through three non-collinear points, in the same way. |
| I 5 | The e-text prints "every point of `a` lies in `a`"; the last `a` is `α`. The two points are distinct: with `A = B` the axiom would put every line through a point of `α` into `α`. |
| I 6 | "Two planes" are two distinct planes, the weaker reading. |
| I 7 | Three clauses: `AxiomI7Line`, `AxiomI7Plane`, `AxiomI7Space`. The space clause is read literally, "there are four points not lying in a plane" (`¬Coplanar A B C D`), which holds when there is no plane at all. Hilbert means four points in general position. The literal reading is kept, because no axiom is strengthened; its consequences are listed below. |
| II 1 | Literal, `Collinear A B C → Between A B C → Between C B A`, with no distinctness clause. |
| II 2 | The two points are distinct. That the new points lie on the line is not stated by Hilbert and follows from `BetweenOnLine` and I 2. |
| II 3 | For three distinct collinear points, at least one and no two of the three betweenness statements hold. Later editions keep only the uniqueness half. |
| II 4 | "Can be so arranged" is a permutation `σ` of `Fin 4`; the four points are distinct. The axiom is redundant (`s3.II4_dependent`). |
| II 5 | Pasch's axiom, with an inclusive "or". That it is exclusive is a theorem. |
| III | `∃!` a line through `A` that does not meet `a`. The two assertions are `AxiomIIIExists`, `AxiomIIIUnique`, and `axiomIII_iff` proves that III is their conjunction (`s5.two_assertions`). |
| IV 1 | Two clauses, `AxiomIV1LayOff` and `AxiomIV1Refl`. "A given side of `A'` on `a'`" is given by a point `C' ≠ A'`: there is exactly one `B'` with `SameSidePt A' C' B'` and `AB ≡ A'B'`. |
| IV 2 | The e-text prints `AB ≡ A'B` for `AB ≡ A'B'`. |
| IV 3 | "No points in common aside from `B`" is `SegmentsMeetOnlyAt A B C`, stated with the closed segments: Hilbert's "points of a segment" never include `B`. This reading, the reading with open segments and `Between A B C` are proved equivalent from II 1–4 (`IV3_reading`, `IV3_reading_open`, unit `s6.IV3_reading`). |
| IV 4 | Laying off: the assigned side of `a'` is given by a point `D'` off `a'`; there is `B'` off `a'` with `∠AOB ≡ ∠A'O'B'` and every interior point of `∠A'O'B'` on the side of `D'`; uniqueness is that of the half-ray `ray O' B'` as a set. In space everything is restricted to the plane `α'`. Reflexivity is the separate clause `AxiomIV4Refl`. |
| IV 5 | For genuine angles. No plane is involved, so the axiom is shared by plane and space geometry, although Hilbert counts IV 4–6 among the plane axioms. |
| IV 6 | Both remaining angles are concluded, as printed. The second conclusion is redundant (`axiomIV6_of_first`, unit `intro.independent`). |
| V | A sequence `A : ℕ → Point` with `A 0` Hilbert's `A`; `A 1` between `A 0` and `B`, `A (n+1)` between `A n` and `A (n+2)`, and the segments `A n A (n+1)` pairwise equal, the weakest reading of "equal to one another". The conclusion is `∃ n, Between (A 0) B (A n)`. |
| Completeness | "Adding elements" is an extension: a `SpaceEmbedding` consists of injective maps on points, lines and planes that preserve and reflect incidence, betweenness and both congruences. `SpaceComplete P L Pl` says that every extension to a structure in the same universes satisfying `SpaceGeometry` is surjective on points, lines and planes; `PlaneComplete` is the plane form. The old system is not assumed to satisfy the axioms in the definition; preservation is required on all tuples, the junk ones included, which is no restriction; surjectivity is required on every sort. The axiom is not a field of any class: a result that uses it names it as a hypothesis, and only `s8.bolzano` and `s8.real_numbers` do. It is satisfiable: `CC.spaceComplete_coord_real` proves it for the analytic geometry over `ℝ`. |

### Degenerate structures admitted by the classes

Two structures that Hilbert's words exclude satisfy the Lean classes. Both are documented as units, and the theorems that fail for them carry an explicit hypothesis.

- **A line without planes is a space geometry.** Under the literal reading of I 7, the real line with no plane satisfies all of `SpaceGeometry`: `coord_one_spaceGeometry` (unit `intro.literal_I7`). Results that need a plane assume one, as `[Nonempty Pl]` or as a given plane: `theorem18`, `axiomII4_of_others_space`, `exists_real_correspondence_space`, `SpaceCoordinatesClaim`, `IdealElementsClaim`, `AnyDimensionClaim`. The hypothesis cannot be dropped: `axiomII4_not_of_others_without_planes` gives a line without planes that satisfies I 1–7, `BetweenOnLine`, II 1–3 and II 5 but not II 4, and `spaceCongruenceGivesTheorem18_false` refutes Theorem 18 on such a line. The models used for consistency are not of this kind (`omega_space_has_plane`, `nonEuclidean_has_plane`).
- **One point and no line is a plane geometry.** `ExistsNoncollinear` asks for three points not on a common line and does not ask that they be distinct, so the structure with one point and no line satisfies `PlaneOrder`, `PlaneParallels` (`s30.degenerate`) and `PlaneCongruence` (`onePt_planeCongruence`). This is a property of the encoding, not an error of Hilbert's, whose three points "not lying in the same straight line" are distinct. Statements that fail there carry a hypothesis: `[Nonempty L]` in `theorem35` and `theorem36_plane'`, and `A ≠ B` for a triangle `ABC` in `theorem20`, `angleSumEqTwoRight_of_parallels` and `noParallelsTheoremCorrected`.

### House rules

- A result assumes exactly the axiom classes Hilbert uses for it, or fewer, never more.
- No axiom is strengthened. A later file that needs a stronger form derives it.
- No `sorry`, `admit`, `native_decide`, `axiom`, `implemented_by` or `unsafe`. `scripts/AxiomsAudit.lean` reports an error if a declaration of the namespace `HilbertFoundations` depends on an axiom other than `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
- The declarations of a unit follow its block.

### Third-party code

Apart from mathlib, three proofs of the formalization rest on code that was not written for it. That code reaches the package in two different ways: the files of `JordanPick/` are copied into the repository, and the library Tau Ceti and the package `hilbert17` are dependencies that Lake fetches, of which the repository contains nothing.

**The copied library `JordanPick`.** The directory `JordanPick/` is the only code in the repository that was not written for it. It holds four Lean files of the repository <https://github.com/rkirov/jordan_pick> by Rado Kirov, under the Apache License 2.0, copied without change from the commit `7905cd4`: `JordanCurve.lean`, `JordanCurve/Arcs.lean`, `JordanCurve/Brouwer.lean`, `JordanCurve/Counting.lean`. They prove the Jordan curve theorem by Maehara's method, from the Brouwer fixed point theorem for the plane, which they prove as well. [`JordanPick/README.md`](../JordanPick/README.md) gives the commit, the checksums of the files and what differs from the upstream repository; [docs/SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) gives the references.

- **Use.** The files are built as a second library of the package, `JordanPick`, and are imported in one place: `HilbertFoundations/JordanCurveClose.lean` derives from them the statement `JordanCurveTheorem` of the unit `app.jordan_theorem` (see "Proved with a copied library" below). No other unit depends on them.
- **Rules.** The unit discipline and the house rules above are those of `HilbertFoundations/`; the copied files keep their upstream form, with their own namespace (`JordanCurve`) and no unit blocks. A search of the four files finds no `sorry`, `admit`, `native_decide`, `implemented_by` or `unsafe` outside the comments, and no `axiom` declaration.
- **Axiom audit.** `scripts/AxiomsAudit.lean` examines the declarations whose names begin with `HilbertFoundations`. The declarations of `JordanPick` have names beginning with `JordanCurve` and are not examined one by one. For each declaration it examines, the script calls `Lean.collectAxioms`, which collects the axioms of everything the declaration depends on, in whatever namespace. The audit of `HilbertFoundations.Appendix.jordanCurveTheorem` therefore covers the part of `JordanPick` that its proof uses: the five statements on the components of the complement, the Brouwer fixed point theorem, the identifications of the plane and of its circle, and everything these depend on. ## Stated, open here

The 11 units with status `stated (open here)`. Each claim is a `Prop`-valued definition. None is assumed anywhere. Where a theorem takes one of them as a hypothesis, it is an implication between statements, such as `steps_of_main` or `atMostOneMiddle_of_twoFixedPoints`.

### Chapter VII (1)

- **`th.44`, Theorem 44.** *Stated:* `Theorem44`, for every domain `R` generated by finitely many real numbers and every tower of square roots over `R`: all radicands are non-negative real numbers under every embedding of the tower field into `ℂ` that is real on `R` (`RealTowerOver`, the reading of "exactly `2ⁿ` real solutions for every position of the given points") iff all numbers of the tower lie in `Ω(R)`. *Proved:* the necessity for every `R` (`condition_necessary`); both directions without parameters (`theorem44_without_parameters`); both directions when the co-ordinates of the given points lie in `ℚ(p)` for one real number `p`, which is the case Hilbert demonstrates (`theorem44_one_parameter`, `theorem44_single_parameter`), and for algebraic co-ordinates (`theorem44_algebraic`); the equivalence `Theorem44For R ↔ Theorem43For R` for every `R` (`theorem44For_iff`, `theorem44_iff_generalized_theorem43`). *Missing:* `Theorem43For R` for two or more independent parameters, or for co-ordinates that are algebraic and not rational functions of one parameter. This is Artin's solution of Hilbert's seventeenth problem with rational coefficients; it needs the Artin–Schreier theory of orderings and the specialization of an ordering of a function field to a real point (Artin–Lang), neither of which is in mathlib. Hilbert leaves the general case open himself (`s39.general_case`).

### Conclusion (2)

- **`concl.pseudo_geometry`, Dehn's pseudo-geometry.** *Stated:* the ideal elements are not defined in the book and are not constructed, so Dehn's theorem cannot be stated in full. `PseudoGeometryTheorem` records its consequences for the real points: for every plane with I, II, IV and every line `t` there are a plane with I–IV and an injective map of the points off `t` into it that preserves and reflects collinearity and makes two lines that meet in a point of `t` parallel. Betweenness is not required to be preserved. *Proved:* nothing. *Missing:* Dehn's theorem (*Math. Ann.* 53).
- **`concl.angle_sum`, the angle sum of one triangle decides that of all.** *Stated:* angles are not added in this edition, so "greater than, equal to, or less than two right angles" is defined through the exterior angle (`ExteriorConfig`, `AngleSumGtTwoRight`, `AngleSumEqTwoRight`, `AngleSumLtTwoRight`). `AngleSumTheorem`: in every plane with I, II, IV, if one triangle falls under one of the three cases, every triangle does. *Proved:* the definitions agree with Theorem 20, in a plane with I–IV every triangle with `A ≠ B` has angle sum two right angles in this sense (`angleSumEqTwoRight_of_parallels`). *Missing:* that the three cases are exclusive and exhaustive, and the theorem; any proof needs a part of neutral geometry (addition of angles, the exterior angle theorem, Saccheri quadrilaterals) that is not developed here.

### Appendix (8)

- **`app.main_theorem`, axioms I–III give the euclidean or the Bolyai–Lobachevskian plane.** *Stated:* `AppendixMainTheorem`, every `Geometry` satisfying `AxiomIGroup`, `AxiomII` and `AxiomIII` is euclidean (`IsEuclideanGeometry`: a bijection of the points onto `ℂ` carries the displacements exactly onto the maps `z ↦ a z + b`, `|a| = 1`) or hyperbolic (`IsHyperbolicGeometry`: a bijection onto the open unit disc carries them exactly onto the maps `z ↦ c (z − a) / (1 − conj(a) z)`, `|c| = 1`, `|a| < 1`). The continuity of the bijection is not required. As stated it is false for the empty plane (`not_appendixMainTheorem`); the statement meant is `AppendixMainTheoremNonempty`. *Proved:* axioms I–III are compatible, and the theorem is not vacuous: the euclidean plane with its proper motions satisfies them and is euclidean in the sense of the theorem (`appendix_axioms_consistent`, `euclideanPlane_isEuclidean`). Four steps of the proof follow from the statement (`steps_of_main`). *Missing:* `AppendixMainTheoremNonempty`, that is, Hilbert's memoir (*Math. Ann.* 56). That Poincaré's disc satisfies axioms I–III is not proved either.
- **`app.invariant_subgroup`.** *Stated:* `AppendixEuclideanTheorem`, a geometry satisfying axioms I–III whose displacements have a normal subgroup other than the trivial one and the whole group (`HasInvariantSubgroup`) is euclidean. *Proved:* the euclidean plane has one, the translations (`euclideanPlane_hasInvariantSubgroup`). *Missing:* the main theorem and the simplicity of the group of proper hyperbolic motions.
- **`app.true_circle_jordan`.** *Stated:* the configuration of Hilbert's outline is not specified, so its intermediate claims are not stated; the conclusion is `TrueCirclesAreJordan`, in a geometry satisfying I–III every true circle is the range of a closed Jordan curve. *Proved:* every true circle is closed in the plane, from axiom III alone (`trueCircle_closed`); in the euclidean plane the true circles are the round circles (`euclideanPlane_trueCircle`). *Missing:* "in itself dense", and the claim.
- **`app.rotation_group`.** *Stated:* `RotationsOfTrueCircle`, for a true circle `k` about `M` some bijection of `k` onto the unit circle conjugates the action of the rotations about `M` exactly onto the rotations `w ↦ c w`, `|c| = 1`. *Proved:* a rotation about `M` maps `k` into itself (`trueCircle_mapsTo`); property (2) (`trueCircle_transitive`); property (1) follows from the proposition (`fixes_trueCircle_of_rotationsOfTrueCircle`); the proposition holds in the euclidean plane (`euclideanPlane_rotationsOfTrueCircle`), in every euclidean and in every hyperbolic geometry whose displacements form a group (`rotationsOfTrueCircle_of_isEuclidean`, `rotationsOfTrueCircle_of_isHyperbolic`), hence it follows from the main theorem (`rotationsOfTrueCircle_of_main`). Property (3), the continuity of the group, is not formalized. *Missing:* a proof from axioms I–III.
- **`app.two_fixed_points`.** *Stated:* `TwoFixedPointsIdentity`, in a geometry satisfying I–III a displacement that fixes two distinct points is the identity. *Proved:* the first law of the quotation follows from it (`rotation_fixing_trueCircle_eq_refl`); it holds in the euclidean plane (`euclideanPlane_twoFixedPoints`), in every euclidean or hyperbolic geometry (`twoFixedPoints_of_isEuclidean`, `twoFixedPoints_of_isHyperbolic`), and follows from the main theorem (`twoFixedPointsIdentity_of_main`). *Missing:* a proof from axioms I–III.
- **`app.transitive`.** *Stated:* `DisplacementsTransitive`, any point can be carried into any other by a displacement. *Proved:* in the euclidean plane (`euclideanPlane_transitive`), in every euclidean geometry and in every hyperbolic geometry whose displacements form a group (`transitive_of_isEuclidean`, `transitive_of_isHyperbolic`), and from the main theorem (`displacementsTransitive_of_main`). *Missing:* a proof from axioms I–III.
- **`app.middle`.** *Stated:* the summary does not define "semi-rotation" and "middle"; they are read as a rotation of order two (`IsSemiRotation`) and a centre of a semi-rotation that carries `A` into `B` (`IsMiddle`). `AtMostOneMiddle`: a segment has at most one middle. The second claim, "every smaller segment possesses a middle", needs a comparison of segments that the summary does not define and is not stated. *Proved:* the euclidean case (`euclideanPlane_semiRotation`, `euclideanPlane_middle`, `euclideanPlane_atMostOneMiddle`); `AtMostOneMiddle` follows from `TwoFixedPointsIdentity` (`atMostOneMiddle_of_twoFixedPoints`) and from the main theorem (`atMostOneMiddle_of_main`). *Missing:* `TwoFixedPointsIdentity`.
- **`app.true_line`.** *Stated:* the true straight line is defined (`TrueLineGen`: the points generated from two fundamental points by semi-rotations and middles; `TrueLine` adds the points of condensation). `TrueLineIsCurve`: it is the range of a continuous injective map of `ℝ`. `TrueLinesMeetAtMostOnce`: two true straight lines have at most one point in common. The other steps of the paragraph (tangent true circles, the numbering by dyadic numbers, the congruence theorems for triangles traversed in the same sense) are sketched without statements precise enough to formalize. *Proved:* in the euclidean plane the true straight line lies on the ordinary straight line (`euclideanPlane_trueLine_subset`). *Missing:* both claims.

- A remark of the third kind is a claim about proofs, and it is verified in the only way it can be: no theorem of the chapter assumes the class in question. For example, no unit of Pascal.lean or of §§ 15–17 before `s17.no_archimedes` assumes `Archimedes`.

## Introduction, conclusion and appendix

These three parts are in [`HilbertFoundations/Commentary.lean`](../HilbertFoundations/Commentary.lean) (41 units), with proofs in `CommentaryClose.lean` and its helper modules and, for Jordan's theorem, in `JordanCurveClose.lean`. The conclusion and the appendix are summaries of memoirs by Dehn and by Hilbert, and the book gives no proofs in them.

**Introduction (5 units).** The claims of the opening sentence are tested against the axioms as formalized. "Complete": groups I–V are not categorical (`intro.complete`, reading). "Independent": false for this edition (`intro.independent`, corrected). As worded, the space axioms admit a geometry consisting of one line (`intro.literal_I7`, proved). The other two units are remarks.

**Conclusion (15 units).** Dehn's axiom system is `PlaneCongruence` without `PlaneParallels` and `Archimedes`.
- *Proved as statements about all geometries:* II 1–4 give Dehn's clauses on the separation of pairs of points, in corrected form (`concl.dehn_order`); a plane with I, II, IV always has parallels (`not_noParallels`, in `concl.no_parallels`).
- *Proved by models:* the non-legendrian geometry (`nonLegendrianGeometryExists`: the part of an elliptic plane infinitely near to a point, over the hyperreal numbers; groups I, II, IV, infinitely many parallels, every triangle with angle sum greater than two right angles); the semi-euclidean geometry (`semiEuclideanGeometryExists`: the finite part of the plane over `Ω(t)`; infinitely many parallels, angle sum two right angles, similar triangles, equal perpendiculars with collinear ends; it is non-archimedean, `semiEuclidean_not_archimedean`); hence Legendre's theorem needs Archimedes's axiom (`legendreNeedsArchimedes`) and the axiom of parallels cannot be replaced by the propositions listed (`parallelsNotReplaceable`). "All the theorems of Riemann's geometry are valid" and "all of the theorems of the euclidean geometry are valid" are formalized only through the properties listed.
- *Stated, open:* the pseudo-geometry and the theorem on the angle sum (`concl.pseudo_geometry`, `concl.angle_sum`).
- *Not constructed:* the elliptic plane, and Dehn's own axioms of order and congruence as an axiom system. The geometry of Lobachevsky is not used for Dehn's table in Commentary.lean; a hyperbolic model, the Cayley–Klein geometry over `ℝ`, is the model of `s10.non_euclidean` in Independence.lean.
- The other 8 units are remarks.

**Appendix (21 units).** The appendix summarizes Hilbert's memoir on the foundations of geometry from groups of displacements.
- *Definitions (7 units).* The number plane is `ℂ`. A Jordan curve is `IsJordanArc`, a closed one `IsClosedJordanCurve`, and a Jordan region `IsJordanRegion`, a bounded connected component of the complement of a closed Jordan curve; the definition does not depend on Jordan's theorem. A plane is a set `S ⊆ ℂ` in which every point has a domain and, for any two points, some domain of the first contains the second (`IsDomainOf`, `IsPlane`); a domain of a point is a Jordan region in `S` and is required to contain the point. The sense of traversal is measured by winding numbers (`WindsAround`, `SensePreserving`). A `Geometry` is a plane with a set `D` of permutations of it, each of which is a displacement (`IsDisplacement`: a continuous, sense-preserving bijection). Axiom I is closure under composition (`AxiomI`); Hilbert's short form, "the displacements form a group", is `AxiomIGroup`, and the main theorem is stated with it. Axiom II is `AxiomII`, with the true circle `TrueCircle` (`axiomII_iff_trueCircle`). Axiom III is `AxiomIII`, with `NearlyBrought` for "triangles arbitrarily near `ABC` can be brought arbitrarily near `A'B'C'`". The footnote to axiom III, on a weakening used in the proof, is not formalized.
- *Proved (2 units).* The three particular cases of axiom III follow from it (`app.special_cases`). Jordan's theorem (`app.jordan_theorem`) is proved in `JordanCurveClose.lean`: the theorem itself by the copied library `JordanPick`, the transport to the statement of the unit here (see "Proved with a copied library" above).
- *In the euclidean model only.* That the euclidean plane with its proper motions satisfies axioms I–III, and the euclidean cases of the steps of the proof, are the partial results of the open units.
- *Stated, open (8 units).* The main theorem, the steps of Hilbert's outline and the theorem on the invariant subgroup, listed above. Hilbert's argument uses Jordan's theorem, which is available; the argument itself is not formalized. Four of the steps are proved to follow from the main theorem (`steps_of_main`). The units `app.true_circle_jordan` and `app.true_line` are not obtained in this way: they concern the position of the true circles and true lines in the number plane, while `IsEuclideanGeometry` and `IsHyperbolicGeometry` ask only for a bijection of the points.
- *Remarks (4 units).* Lie's axioms are not formalized, so the claim that Hilbert's axioms follow from Lie's is not stated. The analogous system for space is a conjecture without a precise statement.

## Not formalized

- **Hilbert's proofs as such.** Each proved unit has a complete Lean proof, which follows Hilbert's argument or not; the Lean reading says which. His proofs are not transcribed step by step. The e-text has no figures, only the markers `[Fig. N]`; where a statement depends on a figure, the configuration is reconstructed from the text and the unit says so (Fig. 13 in `s9.rotation`, Fig. 15 in `s11.theorem10_fails`).
- **Hilbert's ellipse geometry of § 23 as a model.** Theorem 33 is proved with the Moulton plane. The facts about the ellipse and the circles through `F` that Hilbert's construction uses are proved, and so is his computation for Fig. 40; that his broken lines satisfy the axioms is not.
- **Projective geometry.** The ideal elements (`concl.pseudo_geometry`), the projective form of Pascal's theorem (`s14.schur`, `s14.pascal_special`) and conics are not formalized. `s17.ideal` is read as the extension of a space geometry with I–V to the analytic geometry over `ℝ`.
- **A syntax of constructions and of propositions about points of intersection.** § 35 is formalized through the co-ordinate expression of a proposition (`s35.identity`, `s35.conclusion`). Theorems 40 and 41 are proved in co-ordinate planes, and the compass is not formalized as an instrument (`s37.compass`).
- **Geometries of more than three dimensions as axiom systems.** They are replaced by co-ordinate geometries (`s30.any_dimension`, `s34.any_dimension`).
- **The independence claims Hilbert does not argue.** The independence of the space axioms I 3–7 and of the axioms within groups II and IV (`s10.mutual_independence`).
- **The memoirs summarized in the conclusion and the appendix**, beyond the statements and models listed above.
- **The translator's preface and the German original.** The preface is kept in `data/foundations.json` and has no unit.
